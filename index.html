About the Project

What Could This Plaza Really Be?

Montefiore Square sits at the intersection of several Harlem neighborhoods, yet it fails to serve as a true gathering place. This project reimagines the plaza as a green wellness destination — a place where residents can rest, connect, and thrive amid shade, plantings, and thoughtfully designed public amenities.

Using AI image generation and iterative design exploration, this proposal develops a vision grounded in community need, site analysis, and sustainable urban design principles drawn from NYC DOT street design guidelines.

The Problem

A Plaza That Doesn't Work for People

Montefiore Square currently functions as a traffic island rather than a community asset. Harsh paving, lack of shade, poor seating, and no programming leave the space vacant even as surrounding streets buzz with activity.

🌳

No Green Cover

Nearly all surfaces are hard pavement with minimal tree canopy, making the space unbearably hot in summer and uninviting year-round.

🪑

Poor Seating

The few existing benches are poorly positioned, uncomfortable, and not oriented toward any social or visual focal point.

🚸

Safety Concerns

Ambiguous pedestrian routes and limited lighting create safety issues, especially at night and for elderly or disabled users.

🎭

No Programming

There is no dedicated space for markets, performances, or community events — opportunities that could activate the plaza daily.

♿

Accessibility Gaps

Uneven surfaces and unclear pathways make navigation difficult for wheelchair users and people with mobility impairments.

🌧️

No Stormwater Management

Impervious surfaces cause runoff issues. No rain gardens or bioswales exist to manage water and support ecological function.

Site / Context

Understanding Montefiore Square

Located in East Harlem at the junction of several major routes, the plaza sits within a dense residential and commercial context. Understanding movement patterns, user demographics, and environmental conditions informed every design decision.

Dense Residential Context

Surrounded by apartment buildings with limited private outdoor space, making quality public plazas essential for daily life.

🚇

High Transit Foot Traffic

Multiple bus routes and nearby subway access mean thousands of pedestrians pass through or near the plaza daily.

☀️

South-Facing Orientation

Good solar access in winter, but intense summer heat without shade — making canopy planting a top priority.

👥

Diverse User Community

Elderly residents, families with young children, commuters, and local businesses all have a stake in this space.

Final Proposal

The Green Wellness Plaza

The selected proposal transforms Montefiore Square into a layered green space where nature, rest, and community converge. A grove of native shade trees anchors the center, flanked by flexible seating terraces, a rain garden bioswale, and a small all-weather pavilion for programming. Permeable paving replaces hardscape throughout, and clear sightlines prioritize both safety and social interaction.

Reflection / Conclusion

Human + AI Design Thinking

This project used AI as a creative partner — not a replacement for design judgment. Here is an honest account of what AI contributed and where human thinking was essential.

What AI Helped With

DALL·E rapidly visualized design ideas that would have taken hours to sketch manually. It allowed me to test multiple directions quickly — different planting styles, paving materials, and spatial configurations — and compare them side by side. AI also helped generate written drafts that I then revised and personalized.

Prompt 1 "Redesign this Harlem plaza as a green wellness space with shade trees, native planting, and flexible seating — photorealistic aerial view"
Prompt 2 "Before and after redesign of Montefiore Square NYC — transform hardscape into lush community plaza with bioswale and pavilion"
Prompt 3 "Street-level view of redesigned Harlem plaza with people sitting under trees, warm lighting, movable furniture, accessible pathways"

What Required Human Judgment

AI could not evaluate which design was most appropriate for this specific community. Choosing the green wellness concept over the market plaza required understanding the neighborhood's need for calm, green space — something only human site research and community awareness could determine.

Editing AI-generated content for accuracy, relevance, and tone was also entirely a human task. Every image was selected, cropped, and contextualized by me. The AI generated options; I made decisions.

Ultimately, this project taught me that AI is a powerful tool for rapid ideation, but design quality depends on the human judgment applied before, during, and after the AI is used.
